# YC hackathon winners: verified archive

Research date: 2 November 2026

## Scope and methodology

There is no public, complete archive of every hackathon ever held at a Y Combinator venue. This report therefore separates three different relationships that are often blurred together online:

1. **YC-organized**: Y Combinator itself says it hosted/ran the event.
2. **YC co-hosted**: an official organizer says the event was co-hosted with YC.
3. **Partner-run at YC HQ**: a company or group organized the event at YC's office, often with YC providing the venue, a judge, or a guaranteed interview. These are commonly marketed by participants as “YC hackathons,” but they are not necessarily YC-operated.

I searched YC's blog, `events.ycombinator.com`, organizer sites and recaps, official Devpost pages, official company posts, and winner posts. Rankings below are shown only when a source explicitly identifies the placement. Ties, prize tiers, and category winners are not silently converted into a numeric podium.

Confidence:

- **High**: exact placement and project are stated by YC or the official organizer.
- **Medium**: placement is stated by a winner/judge and corroborated by an official event page or another participant.
- **Low**: incomplete or single-source evidence; included only to document a gap.

## Strategic patterns across the winners

These observations are inferences from the verified projects, not official judging rules for the Naïve event.

1. **The product can be understood in one sentence.** “Malaria test from a phone,” “shared paths for browser agents,” “pricing assistant for furniture retailers,” and “support agent that gets cheaper with every ticket” are immediately legible.
2. **The demo contains an observable transformation.** Successful entries turn blood imagery into a diagnosis, live conversation into completed actions, a browser task into a reusable route, reviewed engineering work into a better model, or logistics data into dispatch decisions.
3. **Specificity beats generic horizontal wrappers.** Many podium projects target one workflow and one buyer: chronic-care pre-visits, HVAC contractor leads, independent furniture pricing, freight dispatch, hardware engineering review, or support tickets.
4. **A quantified proof point is unusually powerful.** Examples include 29/30 held-out engineering changes after training, 66% lower support-ticket cost, sub-second route lookup, or a real external workflow being completed.
5. **Judge/sponsor alignment matters when it is product-deep.** The strongest sponsor integrations are essential to the product, not a last-minute API badge.
6. **Memorability comes from the mechanism, not decorative UI.** Adversarial browser agents fighting on live sites, a phone-based malaria microscope, a football helmet with impact sensors, and a live overhearing agent all make the technical thesis visible.

## Coverage limits

- `events.ycombinator.com` does not provide a complete public historical index, and search engines do not reliably index expired event pages or winner images.
- Many recent events were **held at YC** but organized by YC-backed companies. I did not label them “YC-organized” without explicit first-party evidence.
- Some organizers announce winners only in images or videos. When readable text did not identify a project, I left the entry blank instead of guessing.
- Some events award multiple tracks without an overall podium. Those category winners are preserved as categories.
- This archive is strongest for YC's own 2014–2019 events and the publicly documented 2024–2026 SF events. It is not proof that no other YC-venue hackathons occurred.
